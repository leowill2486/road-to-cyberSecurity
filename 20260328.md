## Network Topologies: Critical Failure Points

Today, I explored several common network topologies. Unsurprisingly, no system is without its weaknesses.It reminds me of investment analysis: no company is invincible; they all carry risks.

| Topology | Critical Weakness | Failure Scenario |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bus** | **The Backbone** | If the main cable breaks, the entire network fails due to signal reflection. |
| **Star** | **The Central Hub** | If the central switch/router fails, all connected devices lose communication. |
| **Ring** | **The Sequential Link** | A single break in the cable or one node failure stops the data "token" from passing. |
| **Tree** | **The Root Node** | The higher the failure (e.g., core switch), the larger the branch that goes dark. |
| **Mesh** | **Complexity/Cost** | Nearly invincible physically, but fails due to high cost or configuration loops. |

> **Summary in a Nutshell:**
> - **Bus** fails by the **Line**.
> - **Star** fails by the **Point**.
> - **Ring** fails by the **Link**.
> - **Tree** fails by the **Root**.
> - **Mesh** fails by the **Cost**.

Why Study Network Fundamentals?

Learning protocols like ARP, DHCP, and Subnetting about understanding Systemic Risk.

Risk Isolation: Subnetting is the "Portfolio Diversification" of IT. It prevents a single local failure from cascading into a global collapse.

Verification vs. Trust: ARP and DHCP reveal the inherent flaws in "trust-based" systems. In both networking and finance, unverified trust is the greatest vulnerability.This is something I am currently a bit vague on, and I intend to focus on studying it in depth later.Don't trust, verify？

Architectural Resilience: By identifying Single Points of Failure (SPOF) in topologies, we learn to build "Anti-fragile" systems—whether we are designing a corporate network or an investment strategy.
